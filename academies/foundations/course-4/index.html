Foundations Academy · Lesson 4

Professionalism & Culture

Culture is not a poster on a wall. It is the sum of every choice a Team Member makes in front of a Member, a Guest, a Pet, and each other.

Estimated time 45–60 min Includes value flip cards, a Hannah Translator game, a role-based uniform guide, scenario coaching, a final assessment, and facilitator sign-off.

Lesson objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain what professionalism means at Hannah and why every interaction matters.
  • Describe Hannah's culture, mission, vision, and values in your own words.
  • Use Hannah language and terminology correctly when speaking with Members and Team Members.
  • Meet the professional appearance standards that apply to your role.
  • Communicate professionally in person, by phone, by email, and with your Team.
  • Demonstrate accountability and follow-through on the commitments you make.
  • Contribute to teamwork across Medical, Service, Membership, and Hannah Team Support.
Prerequisites: Lessons 1–3 of the Foundations Academy. This lesson assumes you already understand the Hannah model, Total Lifetime Care®, and how Hannah locations and Teams fit together.

Module 1

Living the Hannah Culture

Every Interaction Matters
Professionalism at Hannah is not a dress code or a script. It is how we treat Members, Guests, Pets, and one another — every time, including when no one is watching.

Culture is carried by people, not documents.

A Member forms an impression of Hannah in the first few seconds of an interaction, and that impression is built from small things: whether someone made eye contact, whether a phone was answered warmly, whether a promised call actually happened, whether two Team Members spoke about each other with respect within earshot.

Every Hannah role — Medical, Service, Membership, Behavior & Training, Facilities, Hannah Team Support — carries the same culture. The tasks differ. The standard does not.

Module 2

Hannah Culture

Shared standard
Culture is what we do consistently — not what we intend to do.
Mission

To provide Total Lifetime Care® for Pets through a Membership model that makes proactive, preventive veterinary care accessible and continuous.

Vision

A world where every Pet receives lifelong care from a Team that knows them, and no Member has to choose between their Pet's health and their finances.

Values

Compassion, Accountability, Respect, Ownership, Communication, Integrity, Teamwork, and Excellence — applied by every role, in every location.

One Hannah

Members should experience one connected organization regardless of which location or Team serves them. Internal structure is never the Member's problem to solve.

Continuous Improvement

We expect to get better. Raising a concern, suggesting a change, or admitting a mistake is treated as contribution, not criticism.

Member Experience

Every touchpoint — a phone call, an arrival, a discharge, a follow-up — is part of one experience that we own together.

Module 3

The Hannah Language

Why words matter
The words we choose tell a Member whether they are a customer being processed or a partner in their Pet's lifelong care.
Compassion

"Member" and "Pet Parent" acknowledge a relationship. "Owner" and "client" describe a transaction.

Consistency

When every Team uses the same words, a Member hears one organization instead of several.

Professionalism

Precise language signals that we are careful — with words, and therefore with Pets.

Brand Identity

Hannah terminology is part of what makes Hannah recognizable and distinct from traditional practices.

Appearance Standards

Hair

Clean and controlled. Long hair tied back when working with Pets or in clinical areas.

Makeup

Natural and understated, appropriate for a medical setting.

Jewelry

Minimal. Nothing that can catch on a Pet, a cage door, or equipment.

Piercings

Conservative and limited. Nothing that creates a safety or handling risk.

Tattoos

Permitted when not offensive. Follow your leader's guidance for your role and area.

Fragrance

Light to none. Strong scents distress Pets and affect Members with sensitivities.

PPE Compatibility

Appearance choices must never prevent correct use of masks, gloves, eye protection, or gowns.

Module 5

Professional Communication

Shared standard
A Member should never have to work hard to be understood, or wonder what happens next.
Positive language

Say what we can do. "Let me find the soonest opening" lands very differently than "we're booked."

Tone

Warm and steady, especially when a Member is upset. Tone carries further than wording — including on the phone.

Active listening

Let the Member finish. Reflect back what you heard before responding, so they know they were understood.

Greeting Members

Acknowledge every Member within a few seconds of arrival, even if you cannot help them yet.

Phone & email etiquette

Identify yourself and Hannah, confirm the Pet and the need, and state clearly who will follow up and when.

Internal communication

Write for the next Team Member: name, Pet, concern, urgency, what has happened, and what remains.

Module 6

Accountability & Follow-Through

Guiding principle
Own the Outcome. Share the Work.

At Hannah, every Team Member is accountable for helping ensure the best outcome for our Members and Pets. You don't have to solve every problem yourself—but you are responsible for making sure the right Team or Team Member receives the information needed to continue the Member's journey.

Taking accountability

Name what happened without softening it. "I missed the callback" opens a solution; "the callback didn't happen" hides one.

Following through

If you promised it, it is yours until it is done or formally handed to someone else who accepted it.

Proactive communication

Tell the Member before they have to ask. A delay explained early is an inconvenience; discovered late it is a broken promise.

Escalating concerns

Raise safety, medical, legal, or ethical concerns promptly. Escalation is a professional act, not a complaint.

Warm handoffs

Share the Member's name, the Pet, the concern, urgency, what has already occurred, and who owns the next step.

Teamwork

Sharing the work means asking for help early — not after the deadline has already passed.

Your commitment
