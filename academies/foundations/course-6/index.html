Hannahware Basics

Hannahware is the practice-management and medical-record system used across Hannah Hospitals for scheduling, Member and Pet information, and charting. Nearly every task in your day passes through it. This lesson is about the judgment that surrounds that work — knowing what the system is for, why accuracy and timeliness matter, and which part of the record is yours to own.

Estimated time 25–35 min Four modules covering navigation habits, Member information and confidentiality, the check-in sequence, and the PetNurse and Nurse Aide charting partnership.

Lesson objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Describe what Hannahware is used for across a Team Member's day, and apply the core habits that keep work in the system accurate
  • Explain what Member information includes, why its accuracy matters, and how confidentiality governs your access to it
  • Sequence the check-in workflow and recognize when to pause and verify before proceeding
  • Describe what belongs in a Pet's medical record, and apply the PetNurse and Nurse Aide division of labor when charting
How this lesson works with your onboarding. This lesson builds the concepts, responsibilities, and judgment you need to work in Hannahware well. The hands-on system training — where things live, how your location has things configured, and the exact steps for your role's tasks — happens alongside your manager or mentor during your onboarding shifts, on the live system, with a real workflow in front of you. That is by design: judgment first, then muscle memory.
Prerequisites: Lessons 1–5. Lesson 5's security and confidentiality module carries directly into Module 2 of this lesson. Like Lesson 5, this lesson uses embedded knowledge checks inside each module rather than a single final assessment, and closes with a short reflection.

Module 1

Hannahware Navigation

The habit that prevents the most rework
Find the right record first. Everything after that depends on it.

Hannahware is where the hospital's day is organized and remembered.

Across a shift, Team Members use it to find a Member or a Pet, view a Pet's record and history, see and manage what is scheduled, and document the care that was delivered. Different roles spend time in different parts of it, but the underlying pattern is the same: locate the right record, read or enter information accurately, confirm the entry is saved, and close out cleanly when you are done.

What Team Members Use It For

Finding a Member or Pet

Looking up who is in front of you, or who is calling, before starting any task.

Viewing a Pet's record

Reading history, prior visits, and existing plans so your work builds on what came before.

Scheduling

Seeing what is booked, what has arrived, and what the day still holds for the care team.

Documenting care

Entering what was observed, done, and planned so the next Team Member has the full picture.

A note on navigation specifics. Systems like Hannahware typically group information by Member and then by Pet, with scheduling and records reached from there — but the exact layout, labels, and paths you will use are shown to you on the live system during onboarding. What follows are the habits that hold true regardless of where a given screen sits.

If you are unsure, stop. Hannahware is a medical record. A guess entered into it does not stay a guess — it becomes history that other Team Members rely on. Asking is always faster than correcting.

Module 2

Member Information

Why a phone number is a clinical detail
If we cannot reach a Member, we cannot deliver the care their Pet needs.

At a high level, Member information covers who the Member is and how Hannah works with them: contact details, membership and plan status, the Pets in the household, and communication preferences. It is administrative on its face and clinical in effect.

What Member Information Includes

Contact details

How Hannah reaches the Member — for appointment reminders, results, and time-sensitive decisions about their Pet.

Membership and plan status

What the Member's plan covers, which shapes what you can tell them about services and cost.

Household Pets

Every Pet associated with the Member, so the right record is opened and the right Pet receives care.

Communication preferences

How and when a Member has asked to be contacted — following it is part of how we show respect.

Why Accuracy Matters

  • Medical decisions. A Pet's care can stall while we try to reach a Member on a number that is out of date
  • Billing. Incorrect plan or contact information produces charges and statements that are wrong, then a conversation nobody wanted
  • Appointment reminders. Reminders that never arrive become missed visits and delayed care
  • Member trust. A Member who has corrected the same detail three times has learned something about how carefully we listen
Carrying Lesson 5 forward — confidentiality. Member information is protected. Access only what your current task requires, and never share Member information outside approved Hannah channels — not with family, not with friends, not with someone who says they already know the Member. The fact that information is available to you is not the same as permission to use or share it.

Module 3

Scheduling and Check-in

What the schedule actually is
A plan for putting the right care, the right Team Members, and the right resources in the same place at the same time.

The scheduling side of Hannahware exists so the hospital's day is organized rather than improvised. An appointment is not only a time — it carries an expectation of what will happen, how long it will take, who needs to be available, and what room or equipment is required. When the schedule is accurate, the care team can prepare. When it is not, the day absorbs the difference.

What an Appointment Coordinates

Time

When the Member and Pet are expected, and how much of the day the visit needs.

Type of care

What the visit is for, which determines preparation and the length of the appointment.

Team Members

Who needs to be available, including any role required for that specific type of care.

Resources

Rooms, equipment, and diagnostics that have to be free at the same time.

Check-in, Conceptually

Check-in is the moment the plan meets reality. Its purpose is to confirm that who arrived matches what was scheduled, to make the arrival visible to the care team, and to surface anything unusual before the visit proceeds. The specific steps you take in the system are shown to you during onboarding; the order of the thinking does not change.

  • Confirm the Member and the Pet who actually arrived
  • Confirm the appointment details match what was expected
  • Update the arrival status so the care team knows the Pet is here
  • Notify the care team that the Pet is ready
Flag anything unusual before proceeding. A Pet who is not on file, a different Pet than the schedule shows, an appointment type that does not match what the Member expects, or a visit that looks double-booked — these are all reasons to pause and verify with the care team or your manager. Working around a mismatch quietly is how a Pet ends up receiving the wrong visit.

Module 4

Medical Record and Charting

What the record is for
The chart is how a Pet's care continues when you are not the one delivering it.

A Pet's medical record is the continuous account of that Pet's health at Hannah. It is read by Team Members who were not present, sometimes years later, and it is the basis on which the next decision gets made. Charting is not administrative overhead attached to care — it is part of the care.

What Belongs in the Record

History

What the Member reports, prior conditions, and what has changed since the last visit.

Exam findings

What was observed during the examination, described objectively.

Diagnostics

What was ordered, what was performed, and what the results showed.

Treatment plan

What was done, what was prescribed or administered, and the reasoning behind it.

Follow-up notes

What needs to happen next, by when, and what the Member was told to watch for.

Member communication

What was explained and agreed to, so the next Team Member does not contradict it.

A Two-Person Job

Charting during an examination at Hannah is a partnership with an established division of labor: the PetNurse examines the Pet and verbalizes findings out loud; the Nurse Aide performs the Hannahware data entry. Neither half works without the other. The PetNurse's findings only reach the record if they are spoken clearly and completely, and the Nurse Aide's entry is only accurate if it captures what was actually said.

This is why reading back matters. If a finding was unclear, ambiguous, or spoken while you were still entering the last one, ask for it again before you enter it. A Nurse Aide who asks the PetNurse to repeat a value is doing the job correctly. A Nurse Aide who guesses at it is not.

Principles of Good Charting

  • Enter it promptly. Chart during or immediately after the visit, not from memory hours later. Memory edits itself; the record should not
  • Be objective and specific. Record what was observed and measured rather than an impression. "Left ear reddened, Member reports scratching for three days" carries information that "ear looks bad" does not
  • Never alter another Team Member's entry. If something is wrong, follow the proper correction procedure so the change and its reason are both visible. Silently overwriting someone's entry destroys the record's reliability
  • Treat every entry as continuity of care. Write for the Team Member who will read it next, without the benefit of having been in the room
